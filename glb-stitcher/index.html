    📖 How to use GLB Stitcher

    GLB Stitcher turns several animated GLB clips into one longer video — over a flat backdrop or a real 3D scene, with multiple characters, transitions, audio, titles and captions. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

    Quick start

    1. Drop your GLB clips into panel 1 (or click to browse). Number the filenames (clip_01, clip_02…) so they order correctly; use ▲▼ to reorder by hand.
    2. Pick a background: panel 2 (2D Scenery) for a flat image/rolling backdrop, or panel 2b (3D Scenery) for a room or open expanse.
    3. Press Preview playback to check it, then Render video (panel 5) to save an MP4.

    Clips & the timeline

    • Each clip row has quick controls: 1× speed pill (click for a slow-mo/fast-forward slider), 💬 caption, 🎥 per-clip camera, 🏃 world-scroll on/off, ⧉ duplicate, ▲▼ reorder, ✕ delete.
    • The timeline strip under the preview shows each character's clips left→right. Click a block to jump the playhead to that clip's start — works while playing (it keeps playing) or paused.
    • Scrub bar = the playhead: drag it to pose the whole stitched scene at any moment; ▶ / 🔁 play & loop.

    Characters & “Show all”

    • Clips are auto-grouped by skeleton — each character gets its own colour and timeline lane.
    • ▦ Show all puts every character on stage together (ensemble). It keeps the moment you've scrubbed to — it won't snap back to the start.
    • If a character slips behind a wall or prop, its skeleton glows through in cyan so you can find it (preview only — not in the render).
    • ◎ Center on active snaps the camera onto the character whose timeline block is highlighted right now.
    • Drag a character on the ground to place it; right-drag (or Shift-drag) to spin it; use ✥ Move / rotate tools for precise gizmo control.

    Scenery & textures

    • 2D Scenery — a flat image or a rolling procedural backdrop (castle, mountains, grass…). Set a scroll speed to fake forward motion.
    • 3D Scenery — pick from 3D Room (floor + 4 walls + optional ceiling), Open expanse (ground + horizon + sky), 🏃 Runner (a close wall + floor that scroll endlessly — run in place forever), or 🛤 Long room (one big fixed floor + back wall to drop obstacles/props along and run the length past them). Each surface has its own 🖼 Texture library, tiling, Bump and Shine, or drop your own image; ✨ Room sets / ✨ Scene sets give one-click matched environments.
    • Props & obstacles (panel 2c) — drop your own .glb models, or click a built-in obstacle (crates, barrels, cones, hurdles, ramps, street signs, traffic lights, benches, a newsstand, dumpsters, and lots more) to drop it in, then drag it into place. Theme packs (Obstacle course, Desert, Dungeon, Street, Alley, Plaza, …) drop a matched group at once.
    • Grid & snapping (panel 2c) — turn on the ▦ Floor grid (and a ▥ Wall grid on the back wall), each with its own size. ⊞ Snap to grid makes dragged items jump to grid lines; 🧲 Snap to obstacles butts their edges together (drop several sidewalk slabs and they line up end-to-end); with either on, an item dragged near the back wall snaps flush against it. Select an item — or Ctrl+click to add more to the selection (Ctrl+A selects every prop) — and use Ctrl+C/V/X, Ctrl+D, Del, and PgUp/PgDn to raise/lower them; a multi-selection moves, copies, and deletes as one group. Grids show only in the preview, never in the render. Turn on 🧍 Stand on obstacles and your characters rest on the tops of flat props (rings, platforms, crates, sidewalks…) as they travel across them — tick Characters can stand on this on any prop's row to make custom models walkable too, and use 🧍 Put character on top to drop them straight onto it. With Stand-on, props also stack (drop a crate on a platform). 🧱 Solid collision stops characters walking through props or the back wall. Scene edits (add / delete / move / clear / paste) are undoable with Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y.
    • Wall backdrops — on a 3D-scenery wall, open its 🖼 Texture library and pick a Wall backdrops scene (Forest, Shopfronts, Western town, City skyline, Brick row houses, Warehouse). It fills the wall behind your characters; in the Runner scene it scrolls past as they move.
    • One-click scenes (panel 2c) — Western street, City block, Forest trail, Downtown, Old town, Industrial each set the matching wall backdrop and drop a matching set of props in one click (switching to the Long-room scene if needed). Then drag the pieces to arrange.

    Camera

    • Orbit: middle-drag, or hold Alt/Space and drag (or use 🎥 Orbit view). Wheel = zoom. Sliders in panel 3 set padding, height, yaw and vertical position.
    • View cube (bottom-right) snaps to Front/Side/Iso angles; Top looks straight down (great for laying out obstacles on the grid). The cube works even before you load a character.
    • Cinematic camera move (panel 3) eases the camera across the whole video — push-in, pull-out, or drift-orbit.

    Titles, captions & audio

    • 🎬 Titles & captions: fading intro/outro title cards, and per-clip lower-third captions (turn captions on here, then add text with a clip's 💬).
    • Audio: drop an mp3/wav/m4a; it's trimmed to the video length and encoded into the MP4.

    Output & saving

    • Render video writes an MP4 (H.264+AAC in Chrome/Edge). Set resolution, FPS, loops-per-clip, hold and cross-fade first.
    • ⬇ Export sequence as GLB bakes the whole timeline (one character + its animations) into one GLB for Blender/Unity.
    • ⬇ Export scene as GLB saves the arranged layout — obstacles & props, and optionally your characters (current pose, or …with animation for the active character's baked timeline) and the environment (floor/walls/terrain) — as a single GLB you can open elsewhere.
    • 🩧 Transparent background drops the backdrop. Use ⬇ Save current frame as PNG for a cut-out image with alpha (MP4 video can't store transparency — its background renders black, so composite it in your editor or use the PNG).
    • ☰ Menu → Save / Save as… stores all your settings and per-clip options in a .json project. Loading restores everything; re-drop the same GLBs (and audio) and their settings reattach by name.

    Tips

    • Every panel header collapses when clicked (▾) — tuck away what you're not using; your layout is remembered.
    • Nothing leaves your computer — it all runs locally.
